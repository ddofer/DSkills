# Papers

Extra rules for a paper; the rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

- **Sections and headings.** Use the venue's standard sections, even when one is short, and short noun headings. Navigation comes from `\section` and `\subsection` headings (no trailing period), mainly in methods and the appendix.
- **No run-in bold heads.** Papers don't open paragraphs with "**Consistency.** text" or `\paragraph{Consistency.}`, and contributions don't start with a bold phrase; this reads as LLM output. The first sentence of a paragraph carries its topic.
- **Prose flows.** One idea per sentence does not mean a string of short sentences. Join sentences that make one argument with an ordinary connective or a subordinate clause, vary their length, and develop one point per paragraph rather than stacking one-sentence paragraphs. No slogan endings ("Accuracy alone hides this.").
- **Introduction, in this order:** background and the gap, what we do, our contributions. This is the one place a paper does not lead with the finding; the abstract and each results section still do. Keep each part short. Figure 1 shows the main idea plainly.
- **Contributions are claims.** Each contribution is one sentence that states what was found or made, with the number or two a reader would quote. Test it: cover every number and reference in the bullet; it must still say what the contribution is. Detail lives in the section it points to. A contribution that only names a topic ("we study amounts") is not a claim yet.
- **Abstract.** Keep to the venue's word limit and count it. An abstract is not a results table. Give the number a reader would quote for each finding, and none when words say it. Intervals, p-values and secondary estimates go in the results. End on a plain claim.
- **Captions.** The first sentence says what is shown; no claim lead and no bold lead. The text, not the caption, makes the argument.
