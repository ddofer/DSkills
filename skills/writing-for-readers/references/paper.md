# Papers

Extra rules for a paper; the rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

- **Sections and headings.** Use the venue's standard sections, even when one is short, and short noun headings. A paragraph heading names its topic ("Consistency"), not its claim.
- **Introduction, in this order:** background and the gap, what we do, our contributions. This is the one place a paper does not lead with the finding; the abstract and each results section still do. Keep each part short. Figure 1 shows the main idea plainly.
- **Contributions are claims.** Each contribution is one sentence that states what was found or made, with the numbers that size it, usually one or two. Test it: cover every number and reference in the bullet; it must still say what the contribution is. Detail lives in the section it points to. A contribution that only names a topic ("we study amounts") is not a claim yet.
- **Abstract.** Keep to the venue's word limit and count it. No number dumping: a number stays only if the reader needs it to size or weigh the finding; a finding stated in words alone is fine. The rest belong in the results. End on a plain claim.
- **Captions.** The first sentence says what is shown; no claim lead and no bold lead. The text, not the caption, makes the argument.
