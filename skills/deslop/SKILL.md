Target: $ARGUMENTS

These habits mark text as machine-written. Most of them do not make it harder to read, so make the text clear first with `writing-for-readers`. That pass already removes "not X, but Y", significance bolted onto a fact, chat leftovers, em dashes, stacked hedges, vague nouns and a new word for the same thing. Never trade clarity for a tell.

Then read the piece aloud in your head. The test for each sentence: would a blunt, fluent researcher write it this way?

## Strong: one sighting is enough to edit

- **Words that spiked with LLMs:** delve, tapestry, testament, realm, pivotal, intricate, meticulous, multifaceted, underscore, showcase, foster, bolster, garner, seamless, vibrant, transformative; "landscape" and "navigate" used as figures of speech.
- **Stock openers and closers:** "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "At its core", "When it comes to", "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "The journey doesn't end here".
- **Hooks:** "But here's the kicker", "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "Let that sink in".
- **A last line shaped like a quotation:** "In the end, the question isn't what the model can do. It's what we do with it."
- **Decoration:** emoji on headings or bullets, Unicode bold, title-case headings.
- **Sources that can't be found:** a citation, quote or "experts say" with nothing behind it. This one is also an error; check every reference.

## Weak: edit when several stack up

- Threes everywhere: three adjectives, three phrases, three bullets of equal length.
- Every sentence 15 to 25 words and every paragraph the same size. Let length follow content: a short sentence for a short point.
- A bold lead and a colon on every bullet; headings on a short text; a summary after each section.
- Bullets where a paragraph would carry the reasoning.
- Balance as a reflex: "while X, Y", "both have merits", "the truth lies in between".
- Curly quotes where the surrounding text uses straight ones; no contractions in an informal piece.
- In fiction and poetry: weather that mirrors feelings, characters who all talk alike, and the stock mood words (whisper, echo, ache, ember).

## How

- When asked to review, show a short before/after table of the worst cases. When asked to edit, edit, then check that no number, citation or reference was lost.
- Don't trade one habit for another. If every cut clause becomes ", because…", that is the new tell.
- Don't add typos, slang or forced quirks to seem human. What shows a person wrote it is the particular: the real name, the date, the number, the example, and an opinion stated outright.
- Don't promise that the text will pass a detector. Detectors misfire on clear, careful writing.
