# Plain words

Write for a smart colleague who wasn't in the project. Every word should be one they already know, or one you define once in plain words.

## Do

- **One idea per sentence.** Judge by meaning, not punctuation: a sentence should make one point the reader can take in at once. Split where a reader would have to hold two claims; keep a tightly linked pair ("X rises, while Y stays flat") together.
- **Say it the ordinary way.** Use what someone outside the project would call it: "players who want the most shock", not "the high-appetite stratum".
- **Use the field's standard term** ("odds ratio", "95% interval"). Never one you invented.
- **Shorter when just as clear:** "edgier answers win more", not "a positive association between transgression intensity and selection probability".
- **One name per thing, everywhere:** text, tables, figure labels and captions. Three names read as three things.

## Watch for

- **Coined labels** ("regulars", "end fifths", "the dial", "arms", "main set"). Replace them with what they measure.
- **Code in prose:** file names, variables, job ids, internal section labels. Move them to a sources list.
- **Terms seeded by notes.** Project notes, memory and glossaries spread labels into later writing. When you replace a label, fix it there too, and list the old name as one to avoid.
- **Defensive hedging and machine-facing notes.** Cut caveat piles, "to our knowledge", repeated disclaimers, provenance bookkeeping and notes written for the next agent rather than the reader. State a real limit once, beside the claim it limits.
- **Long words where short ones work:** "use", not "utilize"; "to", not "in order to".

## Keep

- **Every number and its meaning.** Plain is not vaguer. Don't change a number while rewording.
- **The user's own terms.**
- **A defined term that saves real space**, defined once where it first appears.
