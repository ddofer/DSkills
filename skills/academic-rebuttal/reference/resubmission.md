# Resubmission plan

Use when rebuttal has low expected return or the author asks for it. Frame resubmission as the higher-return path, not as failure.

1. **Why it was rejected.** Name the main mechanism: unclear contribution, too little evidence, a technical flaw, unfair evaluation, weak novelty positioning, wrong venue audience, overclaiming, poor writing, or several independent concerns with no advocate.
2. **What is still worth answering now** (factual errors), and what not to spend time on.
3. **Preserve / change / remove:**

   | Preserve | Change | Remove or narrow |
   |---|---|---|

4. **Revision backlog**, ranked R0 (blocks resubmission), R1 (strongly recommended), R2 (completeness), R3 (polish):

   | Priority | Revision | Reviews addressed | Work needed | Expected payoff |
   |---|---|---|---|---|

5. **Next-submission experiments:** for each, the hypothesis, concern addressed, protocol, cost, stopping rule, and what a positive, negative or null result would mean.
6. **Story and claims:** a revised one-sentence contribution and abstract claim, the strongest defensible novelty statement, claims to narrow, limitations to put up front, and results to promote, move to the appendix or drop.
7. **Paper structure:** concrete changes to the title, abstract, introduction, related work, method, setup, main tables, analysis, limitations and reproducibility details.
8. **Venue fit**, only when there is enough information to ground it.
